## 1. Quote request intake that creates a clear next step

A quote request should not just land in an inbox and wait for someone to notice it.

A simple intake automation can help by turning each new request into a clear task.

- Trigger: someone fills out a website form, calls after hours, or submits a referral request.
- Action: the system creates a lead record or task with the person’s contact details, request type, and source.
- Human handoff: the assigned team member receives a notification and owns the next step.
- Customer experience: the prospect receives a short confirmation that the request was received and someone will review it.
- When not to automate: do not automate coverage advice, eligibility decisions, or anything that requires licensed review.

This is where a [lead follow-up automation](/services/missed-lead-rescue-system) workflow can be helpful. The automation handles the reminder and routing. Your team still handles the conversation.

## 2. Insurance quote follow-up that does not rely on memory

Open quotes are easy to lose track of during a busy week.

An insurance quote follow-up workflow can create a simple sequence after a quote is sent. For example, a task could be created for the producer two business days later, followed by a polite check-in reminder if the prospect has not responded.

A practical setup might look like this:

- Trigger: a quote is marked as sent in the CRM or tracking sheet.
- Action: a follow-up task is created with a due date.
- Human handoff: the producer or account manager reviews the quote before contacting the prospect.
- Customer experience: the prospect receives a helpful check-in, not a pushy canned message.
- When not to automate: if the quote involves complicated coverage questions, the follow-up should prompt a human call rather than sending a generic message.

## 3. Renewal reminder automation that starts early enough

Renewals can become stressful when the team only notices them at the last minute.

Renewal reminder automation can help create a repeatable process before a policy renews. The workflow might remind the team to review the account, check for missing information, prepare outreach, or schedule a conversation.

For an agency serving both personal lines and commercial clients in Western New York, the workflow should be simple enough that the team actually uses it.

Example: A renewal is 45 days away. The system creates an internal review task, assigns it to the right account manager, and adds a reminder if no action has been logged after a few days. The client only hears from the agency once a person has reviewed what needs attention.

That keeps the workflow human-led while reducing the chance that renewal work piles up unexpectedly.

## 4. Client question routing for common requests

Insurance agencies get repeat questions all the time.

Some are simple administrative questions. Some need a licensed person. Some are urgent. Some can wait until the next business day.

Automation can help sort the request before it becomes another vague inbox item.

A simple routing workflow might:

- collect the client’s name, contact information, and request type;
- separate billing, document, claim, quote, and renewal questions;
- flag urgent or sensitive requests for human review;
- create a task for the right person;
- send a confirmation that the message was received.

The key is to avoid pretending automation can answer everything. For insurance, the safest and most professional approach is often to acknowledge, route, and prepare the handoff.

## Common mistakes to avoid

### Automating too much at once

Start with one workflow that is already repeatable. Quote follow-up, renewal review tasks, or website quote request routing are usually better first projects than a full agency-wide overhaul.

### Sending robotic client messages

Automation should not make your agency sound like a generic call center. Keep messages short, clear, and human. Use automation to create the reminder, then let the team personalize when needed.

### Skipping ownership

A workflow is only useful if someone owns the next step. Every automated task should have a responsible person, a due date, and a clear action.

### Treating automation as licensed advice

Automation should not replace professional judgment, coverage guidance, or compliance review. Use it for intake, reminders, routing, and organization. Keep advice and decisions in human hands.
