## The 10 questions to ask before hiring

1. What exact workflow will you automate first?
2. Which tools, inboxes, forms, CRMs, calendars, phone systems, or spreadsheets will it touch?
3. What happens when the system is unsure, missing data, or dealing with a sensitive reply?
4. Who owns the code, accounts, API keys, data, phone numbers, domains, workflows, and prompts?
5. How will the workflow be documented so my team can understand it later?
6. What monitoring, maintenance, and error response are included?
7. Which claims can you substantiate with examples, references, or a walkthrough?
8. What sensitive data is involved, and how will access be limited?
9. What does the customer experience look like from first message to human handoff?
10. What is the smallest pilot and stop condition if the system is not ready?

The FTC’s business guidance states that advertising claims should be truthful and substantiated ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-made-usa-standard)). Apply that principle to AI automation pitches: ask what each claim means for your workflow, not just for a generic case study.

You do not always need someone physically in Buffalo. Local context can help with meetings, service-area nuance, small-business relationships, and understanding how WNY customers communicate. Regional or remote specialists can still be valid if delivery is documented, support hours fit, and ownership terms are clear.

Platform marketplaces and DIY tools can also fit simple workflows. If the first automation is “form submission creates a task and sends an internal email,” your team may be able to start with a no-code tool. If the workflow touches sensitive data, AI interpretation, customer-facing replies, phone systems, or multiple business tools, getting help may be safer.

Require transparency about office location, support availability, handoff, and maintenance. If a provider claims a local presence, verify what that means.

## Red flags in AI automation pitches

Be cautious when you see:

- “Best,” “top,” or “#1” claims with no independent proof.
- Guaranteed ROI, rankings, booked jobs, or revenue without your data.
- No mention of maintenance, logs, or error handling.
- No human handoff for sensitive work.
- A requirement to replace every current tool before mapping the workflow.
- Unclear ownership of accounts, API keys, data, code, workflows, or prompts.
- No documentation or training.
- Vague compliance language without scope or professional review.
- Public-facing AI content with no approval process.
- Local claims that are not clear on the provider’s own site.
